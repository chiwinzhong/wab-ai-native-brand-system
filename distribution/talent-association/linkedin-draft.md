# LinkedIn draft — WAB Interview-to-Story field case

> Draft only. Not authorized for publication.

A transcript is not a story.

A translation is not localization.

An image prompt is not a visual system.

I have been turning two real editorial patterns in Dongguan—《即莞来》 and 《选择东莞》—into a public Agent Skill:

**WAB Interview-to-Story**

It converts interview audio or notes into:

- a time-coded transcript;
- an evidence and quotation ledger;
- a person-story or place/choice route;
- one approved canonical story;
- natural Chinese and English editions;
- LinkedIn, Substack, X, WeChat, and other channel adaptations;
- visual briefs and brand-ready cards or posters.

The important difference is the order.

Every derivative returns to one evidence-linked story. The system does not translate a LinkedIn post into a Substack article and then ask an image model to invent the rest.

The two routes also use different truth systems:

- A person story depends on the irreplaceable interviewee, source-linked facts, and verified quotations.
- A place/choice story uses current public information as primary evidence; one person's experience cannot prove current city conditions.

Facts, quotations, translation, image rights, and publication each keep a human gate.

The public package now includes the Skill, schema, validator, negative tests, and a fictional example. It does not include raw recordings, unpublished interviews, portraits, internal task data, or performance claims.

The claim I am willing to make today is narrow:

> One interview can become many assets. One approved truth should remain behind all of them.

The next test is a privacy-safe comparison against a prompt-only workflow, measuring unsupported claims, quotation drift, human correction, cross-channel contradiction, and visual rework—not only speed.

Where would this model overclaim?
