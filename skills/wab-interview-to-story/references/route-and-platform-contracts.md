# Story routes and platform contracts

## Route A — Person story

Use `person_story` when replacing the person would make the story collapse.

Primary evidence:

- original interview audio, video, transcript, or notes;
- confirmed identity and biographical facts;
- source-linked direct quotations and paraphrases;
- necessary public context.

Editorial question:

> What did this person choose, how did they judge the situation, and what did that choice cost or change?

Do not use one person's experience as a population claim, promotional endorsement, or proof that a city, organization, or product works for everyone.

The public field case refers to this route through the Chinese series 《即莞来》. The repository exposes the route logic, not private interviews or the complete internal editorial system.

## Route B — Place/choice story

Use `place_choice_story` when the reader is deciding whether to choose a place for work, life, entrepreneurship, or long-term development.

Primary evidence:

- current official or authoritative public sources;
- valid policy, statistics, service, place, and industry information;
- bounded public cases;
- interviews as supporting context, not as substitutes for public evidence.

Editorial question:

> What current, verifiable conditions should this audience understand before choosing this place?

Distinguish:

- completed fact;
- current policy;
- work in progress;
- future plan;
- editorial judgment.

The public field case refers to this route through the Chinese series 《选择东莞》. The repository does not publish private source indexes, internal task records, or unpublished case material.

## Channel contracts

Each channel derivative must identify its language, audience, desired next action, evidence IDs, format, and status.

### Canonical long article

- Purpose: factual and editorial source for all derivatives.
- Typical length: determined by the evidence, not a fixed platform limit.
- Required: central question, route, evidence, boundaries, and source note.
- Prohibited: platform gimmicks, unsupported hooks, and copy inherited from another derivative.

### WeChat article

- Purpose: full Chinese editorial experience.
- Structure: strong but factual opening, useful subheads, complete story, source or rights note.
- Visuals: branded cover plus only the cards or explanations that add information.

### Xiaohongshu post

- Purpose: mobile discovery and saving.
- Structure: concrete reader problem, three to five useful sections, clear boundary, non-inflated title.
- Visuals: 3:4 cover or card sequence only when evidence and brand rules support it.

### LinkedIn post

- Purpose: professional insight and conversation.
- Structure: one sharp operational insight, one field example, one bounded claim, one question.
- Avoid: institutional slogans, raw translation, hashtag walls, or unexplained local context.

### Substack article

- Purpose: durable English field note or theory-building article.
- Structure: real problem, non-obvious judgment, mechanism, field evidence, limitation, invitation to critique.
- Include enough local context for an international reader without turning the place into an exotic backdrop.

### X thread

- Purpose: concise argument and research relationship-building.
- Structure: one claim per post, evidence and limits close to the claim, link only after the argument is clear.
- Avoid: fragmenting a weak article into many posts or presenting an unverified quote as a hook.

### Poster or content card

- Purpose: visual entry point or one verified expression.
- Required: role, channel, aspect ratio, safe zone, evidence IDs, brand rules, rights status, and review status.
- A poster is a derivative of the approved story, not an independent fact source.

## Derivation rule

```text
sources
→ evidence ledger
→ approved route
→ canonical story
├── Chinese platform derivatives
├── natural English re-edit
│   └── international platform derivatives
└── visual brief
    └── channel-specific visual assets
```

Never derive one platform output from another when the canonical story is available.
